# Design checklist

These are checks, not a recipe. They say what a design must satisfy, not how it must look. They
apply to anything that people use or read.

Write every answer down. A check that you did not write down did not happen.

- Part 1 goes in the brief, before any prototype. A second agent reviews the brief.
- Parts 2 to 5 go on the review page: yes or no, and one line of evidence for each.
- The reviewer answers parts 2 to 5 on its own. Where its answer differs from yours, the item is
  "no".

A "no" stops the round. An item may be marked "does not apply" only with a reason that the
reviewer accepts.

## 1. The people and the jobs

- [ ] I wrote who uses the thing, where, with what, and with how much attention.
- [ ] I wrote the jobs it does for them, the most frequent first.
- [ ] Each part serves one of the jobs. I removed the rest.
- [ ] Nothing was added to fill space. If the thing looks empty or thin, I checked the jobs again
      before adding a part.
- [ ] I wrote what the people need here that the thing does not give. Each item is in this round,
      or listed as later with a reason.
- [ ] Each thing that people must enter or decide is needed now. I looked for a default, a later
      step or a smaller ask.
- [ ] I wrote what happens in the hard cases: empty, first use, slow, failing, a lot of content,
      long or odd content.
- [ ] Where the thing has more than one form, each form does the same jobs. I wrote what the
      extra room is for.
- [ ] I looked at how other products do the same jobs, and wrote what I take or reject.

## 2. Structure

- [ ] I can name what people notice first, second and third, and it matches the job order.
- [ ] Related parts sit together, and groups are clearly apart.
- [ ] Each state keeps the same frame; only what changes changes.
- [ ] No part is empty or padded without a reason that I wrote down.
- [ ] Each fact appears once.

## 3. Form

- [ ] Like parts look and behave alike.
- [ ] I used the project's conventions or design system. Each departure has a written reason.
- [ ] Every word is plain, short and the same as elsewhere in the product.
- [ ] It is easy to read: size, contrast, order, and no bad breaks or cuts.
- [ ] Alignment and spacing are even.
- [ ] It works in each mode the product has, for example a theme, a size or an output format.

## 4. Use

- [ ] The main action is easy to find.
- [ ] Each control or command says what it does.
- [ ] I wrote what each action does, and what feedback it gives.
- [ ] Actions that lose work are hard to trigger by accident and ask first or allow an undo.
- [ ] People who use assistive tools, or who cannot use one input, can still do the jobs.

## 5. Verify

- [ ] I have a prototype of every state in every form.
- [ ] I looked at every changed part closely after the last change.
- [ ] Odd content in the prototypes is a case I designed for, not a fault of the test data.
- [ ] Beside work the person accepted, it looks like the same product.
- [ ] As the user from part 1, I can tell where I am and what to do next at once.
