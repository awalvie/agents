# Uncertainty

Read this before you cite a number, or when a finding decides a direction. Labels are in `README.md`.

## Disputed, and what the answer depends on

- Do instructions stop copying? Jansson & Smith (1991) and Smith et al. (1993) say no. Chrysikou & Weisberg (2005) say yes. The review calls it "equivocal". Naming the specific features may be the difference (inferred).
- Look at competitors first? One consultant calls it "polluting your mind" (Crilly 2015), and other designers start with a competitive audit (Herring et al. 2009). Near examples serve checking, and far examples serve generation (inferred).
- Rough or polished work for feedback, options or one answer (Rand's refusal to Jobs is quoted only second-hand), content first or structure first, code or sketch, ratio or hand-picked scales, utility or cascade CSS: `core.md` or the topic file says what each depends on. None has outcome data.
- Typicality or novelty? Tuch et al. (2012) and Silvennoinen et al. (2025) weight them differently. The reconciliation by exposure time in `core.md` is the synthesis's, and untested.
- Structure or surface as the place for distinctiveness? The three-layer split in `core.md` is the synthesis's.

## Weak evidence to keep in mind

- Most fixation and prototyping studies use students, short tasks and sketches or banner ads. Trust the direction of effects more than their size (Vasconcelos & Crilly 2016). The Dow studies come from one group, and their similarity tests treat thousands of paired judgements as independent.
- The aesthetics studies rate static screenshots, mostly at first glance. None measures appeal after long use, or memorability.
- Team case studies (Linear, Duolingo, Mailchimp, Mozilla, GOV.UK, Superhuman, Basecamp) show intent, not outcome.
- The posture framework is one studio's, and the navigation-model lists are untested.
- The claims about AI-generated interfaces rest on two 2026 preprints that are not peer reviewed, and on a vendor's account of its own model.
- The relativity of colour is labelled A as standard perception science, but no paper on it appears in the notes.

## Corrections made in the synthesis

- Etsy (structure notes): the "power users opened results in new tabs" story is a separate Etsy test, in which opening items in new tabs made 70% more people leave. It does not explain the infinite-scroll result (transcript checked).
- Font size (visual notes): the study's 18 pt is 24 px in a browser, and the source does not show that gains stop there (Pielot's summary checked).
- MAYA on websites (languages notes): the stronger finding is that novelty counted for less on commercial sites. The extra weight of typicality there was small, a difference of .017 in effect size, against .185 for novelty (paper checked).
- Hekkert et al. (2003): expertise did not change the balance of typicality and novelty (abstract checked). The languages notes were unsure.
- Tuch et al. (2012): both the 17 ms and 50 ms figures are right. Study 1 used 50, 500 and 1000 ms, and study 2 used 17, 33 and 50 ms; at 17 ms the typicality effect was weaker (paper checked).
- Labels changed: breadth over depth A-B to B; audience differences A to B; a constrained scale from strong heuristic to useful pattern in the code notes, to match the visual notes, which rest on the same sources.

## Gaps

- No source sets the number of directions.
- No study shows whether high-fidelity HTML raises fixation through sunk cost. Evidence on building is mixed: building reduced fixation in Youmans (2011), and sunk-cost attachment appeared in Viswanathan & Linsey (both via Vasconcelos & Crilly).
- No study compares gridded and ungridded layouts, ratio and hand-picked scales, or intrinsic and breakpoint layouts.
- Not covered: game UI, dense East Asian commerce design, Y2K revival. Culture appears only in Reinecke & Gajos (2014).

## Figures not to cite

- Untraceable or false: whitespace raises comprehension by 20% (Lin 2004 did not find it); 60-30-10; golden-ratio beauty; the rule of thirds as a law; 8 px as optimal; 66 characters as ideal on screen; seven plus or minus two menu items; the three-click rule; the *Art & Fear* ceramics class (a retold photography anecdote); GitLab's 402 text colours; "2,300+ viewport sizes".
- No study behind them: Nielsen's "more than two disclosure levels"; "The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee" (not found in the pages read).
- Traceable but not usable as evidence: Aalen's donation rises of 198% and 382% (they do not isolate the change); Apple's "10 to 3 to 1" (second-hand practice); Baymard's "8+ steps" (methods not public); Google's "up to 87%" (company research); Minto's 3.08% (second-hand); Burmistrov's 15.4 s against 8.0 s (confounded by thin type); lab transition times near 1 s as a UI default.
- Cite only with their label: the Design Theater preprint's 25% and 34%, and the Vibe Design Agents preprint's similarity scores.
