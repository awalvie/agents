# Uncertainty

Read this before you cite a number, or when a finding decides a direction. Labels are in `README.md`.

## Disputed, and what the answer depends on

- Do instructions stop copying? Jansson & Smith (1991) and Smith et al. (1993) say no. Chrysikou & Weisberg (2005) say yes. The review calls it "equivocal". Naming the specific features may be the difference (inferred).
- Look at prior work first? Designers disagree (`core.md`).
- Rough or polished work, options or one answer (Rand's refusal to Jobs is second-hand), content or structure first, code or sketch, ratio or hand-picked scales, utility or cascade CSS: the topic files say what each depends on. None has outcome data.
- Typicality or novelty? Tuch et al. (2012) and Silvennoinen et al. (2025) weight them differently. An exposure-time reconciliation fails: Tuch's typicality effect held at 500 and 1000 ms, and Silvennoinen states no viewing time. The difference is unexplained.
- Structure or surface as the place for distinctiveness? The three-layer split in `core.md` is the synthesis's. Its counterexamples in `core.md` are company accounts.
- Persist or pivot? No study says when to drop a chosen direction (`core.md`).

## Weak evidence to keep in mind

- Most fixation and prototyping studies use students, crowd workers, short tasks and sketches or banner ads: Dow et al. 2009 to 2011, Girotra, Kulkarni, Wadinambiarachchi, Tohidi, Findlater. Vasconcelos & Crilly (2016) stress that methods vary widely. Trusting the direction of effects more than their size is the synthesis's advice, not theirs. The Dow studies come from one group, and their "experienced" participants were not professionals.
- The aesthetics studies rate static screenshots, mostly at first glance. None measures appeal after long use, or memorability. Hekkert et al. (2003) rated physical consumer products, not interfaces, and its effects appear only once the other variable is controlled.
- Team case studies (Linear, Duolingo, Mailchimp, Mozilla, GOV.UK, Superhuman, Basecamp) show intent, not outcome.
- The posture framework is one studio's, and the navigation-model lists are untested.
- The claims about AI-generated interfaces rest on two 2026 preprints that are not peer reviewed, and on a vendor's account of its own model. In Zhang et al. an LLM judge mostly preferred the baseline, so the spec-first method has evidence for variety only.
- The relativity of colour is labelled A as standard perception science, but no paper on it appears in the notes.

## Corrections to the research notes

- The Etsy new-tab test is separate from the infinite-scroll test and does not explain it. Rello's paper gives no pixel value. Silvennoinen's .185 and .017 are service-versus-commercial differences within each predictor. Expertise did not change Hekkert's balance. Tuch used 50 to 1000 ms in study 1 and 17 to 50 ms in study 2.
- Label and wording changes made by the skeptic are in `skeptic-log.md`.

## Bias in the base

- One research firm dominates the evidence on interfaces: NN/g and its authors are named more than 20 times across the topic files, often for the only data on a question (hidden navigation, flat design, centred logos, photos). The cited NN/g articles are not peer reviewed.
- The observed exemplars are mostly US and UK web products of the 2010s and 2020s: Stripe, Linear, GOV.UK, Craigslist, The Pudding, Gumroad. Linear appears in `core.md` and three topic files. Treat them as cases, not as the standard.
- Google and Apple are cited on their own design systems (Material 3 Expressive, Liquid Glass, HIG), where they have an interest in the result.
- Western modernist typography and grid practice (Bringhurst, Müller-Brockmann) frame the visual file; no source covers non-Latin scripts.

## Gaps

- No source sets the number of directions.
- No study shows whether high-fidelity HTML raises fixation through sunk cost. Evidence on building is mixed: building reduced fixation in Youmans (2011), and sunk-cost attachment appeared in Viswanathan & Linsey (both via Vasconcelos & Crilly).
- No study compares gridded and ungridded layouts, ratio and hand-picked scales, or intrinsic and breakpoint layouts.
- Not covered: game UI, dense East Asian commerce design, Y2K revival, non-Latin typography, voice and conversational interfaces. Culture appears only in Reinecke & Gajos (2014).
- No study tests when a furniture departure pays off; only company accounts exist.

## Sources that could not be read, or only in part

- Coverage books: Jansson & Smith (paywalled, used through three descriptions); Rosenfeld et al. (search snippets only); Bringhurst (through Rutter's site); Müller-Brockmann (pages 9 to 12); Albers (Foundation quotes); Meggs (table of contents only); *Every Layout* (free chapters only).
- Also unread: Lawson, *How Designers Think*; Tidwell et al. text; Refactoring UI beyond two previews; Hay's *Responsive Design Workflow*; the Hazelrigg critique of Pugh; crowd-critique studies (Krause et al., Yuan et al.); Buckingham Shum & Hammond's conclusions; *Creativity, Inc.*; Minto's font-size post; Imhof and Tufte.
- Many quotes in the notes came through a fetch tool that summarises pages. `sources.md` lists which sources were then read raw. Quotes from the others are unchecked.

## Figures not to cite

- Untraceable or false: whitespace raises comprehension by 20% (Lin 2004 did not find it); 60-30-10; golden-ratio beauty; the rule of thirds as a law; 8 px as optimal; 66 characters as ideal on screen; seven plus or minus two menu items; the three-click rule; the *Art & Fear* ceramics class (a retold photography anecdote); GitLab's 402 text colours; "2,300+ viewport sizes".
- No study behind them: Nielsen's "more than two disclosure levels"; "The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee" (not found in the pages read).
- Traceable but not usable as evidence: Aalen's donation rises of 198% and 382% (they do not isolate the change); Apple's "10 to 3 to 1" (second-hand practice); Baymard's "8+ steps" (methods not public); Google's "up to 87%" (company research); Minto's 3.08% (second-hand); Burmistrov's 15.4 s against 8.0 s (confounded by thin type); lab transition times near 1 s as a UI default.
- Cite only with their label: the Design Theater preprint's 25% and 34%, and the Vibe Design Agents preprint's similarity scores.
