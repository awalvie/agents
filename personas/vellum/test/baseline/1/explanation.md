# Three directions for the shared grocery list

## Direction 1: Aisle checklist (direction-1.html)

One checklist that looks like the paper list people already know, grouped by part of the store so the shopper walks the aisles in order. Every row is large, shows who asked for the item and any note, and turns to "Anna took this" the moment another shopper ticks it. Adding is one text field that warns you when the item is already on the list and shows who asked for it. The tradeoff: it is the easiest to learn, but it stops double buying only if both shoppers watch their screens. Two people can still reach for the same item in the same second.

## Direction 2: Home mode and Shop mode (direction-2.html)

Adding and shopping are different jobs in different places, so the app has two modes. At home it opens on the add box and a "hold to say it" button, so adding while cooking takes a word and a tap. In the store, "Start shopping" gives a dark, high-contrast list with very large cards and every control at the bottom, under the thumb. When a second person starts shopping, the app offers to split the list by aisle, so each person has their own part and double buying cannot happen. The tradeoff: this is the best fit for one-handed shopping and two shoppers, but it adds a mode switch and a split step. Less confident users must learn both, and a fixed split is rigid when one person reaches the dairy aisle first.

## Direction 3: Show me the packet (direction-3.html)

This direction is built around "which cheese?". Anyone can add an item by taking a photo of the empty packet, and the list shows that photo, the face of the person who asked, and a short question-and-answer thread on the item. Ticks show who got the item. The tradeoff: photos and threads answer "which one?" before anyone has to phone home, and they are natural for people who find typing hard. But each row is taller, so the shopper scrolls more. The photo step also makes adding slower than typing one word, so it must stay optional.
