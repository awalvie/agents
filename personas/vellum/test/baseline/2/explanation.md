All three files use the same mock day: six therapists on different hours, 83 appointments, the time is 10:40, two patients are late, and three cancellations have left gaps. Each file opens on the day view. The switch in the bottom corner, or `#booking` at the end of the URL, opens the booking screen. Check-in, payment, move, cancel and booking all work and change the day as you use them.

direction-1.html, the planner grid. This is the paper appointment book on screen: one column per therapist, time running down, off-hours and lunch hatched out, and open gaps drawn as dashed boxes. Everything happens where you point. Click an empty slot to book there. Click a block to check in, take payment, move or cancel. When you cancel, the toast offers "Fill gap". The booking drawer shows a ghost block on the grid, shows each therapist's first free time, and checks Room 3, the gym and the laser unit for treatments that need them. A toggle turns the columns into rooms and machines. The tradeoff: it is the most familiar layout and gives the best overview of parallel time, but it needs the mouse. At 90 appointments the grid is dense, and filling gaps still depends on the receptionist seeing them. The side rail helps with that only partly.

direction-2.html, the keyboard ledger. The day is a dense list sorted by time, with a command line, built for a receptionist who has the phone in one hand. J and K move between rows, and C, P, M, X and U check in, take payment, move, cancel and undo. Finished, paid visits fold away, so the list starts near the current time. Gaps are rows in the list: press G to jump to one, then 1 to give it to the best waitlist match. Booking is a five-step prompt with numbered choices and type-to-filter, or a single line such as `b kari fu tom 1115`. A free-time strip above the list shows where the slot lands. The tradeoff: it is the fastest option for a trained user, with the fewest trips to the mouse. But a list shows parallel time poorly, and the strip makes up for that only a little. The shortcuts must be learned and are hard to discover, which is bad for temporary staff.

direction-3.html, the gap board. The screen is built around what needs doing now, not around the calendar. It has three columns: arriving (late patients first), in the clinic (waiting, in a room, or still to pay), and open time, where each gap has its best waitlist matches with reasons and one-click "text offer" or "book". A late patient's card has "No-show, open the slot", which turns lateness into a fillable gap straight away. Booking is "Find a time": describe what the patient needs and get ranked suggestions with the reasons shown (closes a gap exactly, usual therapist, within what the patient asked for, machine free, leaves a 15-minute hole). Every free time is listed below as a manual fallback. The tradeoff: it has the shortest path from a cancellation to a refill and asks for the fewest decisions, but the desk must trust the ranking. It is also weaker at manual work, such as moving several appointments around or reading a whole afternoon at a glance. The timeline strip at the top is for overview only.
